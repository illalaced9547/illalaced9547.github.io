❓ What Is OpenRelief?

OpenRelief is a humanitarian forecasting tool that helps aid organizations, governments, and researchers anticipate food shortages before they become crises. It combines several types of open data — conflict events, rainfall patterns, market food prices, and shipping logistics — to produce a clear, district-level risk score.

The key word is explainable. Unlike a “black box” AI that just gives a number, OpenRelief shows you why a district is at risk. Was it due to recent conflict? A drop in rainfall? A spike in maize prices? You’ll see the contributing factors visually, making it easier to trust, verify, and act on the forecast.

🎯 Why Use OpenRelief?

  • Early warning: Spot districts trending toward food insecurity weeks in advance.
  • Transparent predictions: Every forecast comes with a simple visual explanation.
  • Open data only: No proprietary datasets — everything is from public sources.
  • Built for humanitarian use: Designed alongside field experts, not just data scientists.
  • District-level granularity: National or regional maps hide local spikes; OpenRelief zooms in.

⚙️ Key Features

📊 Multi-Source Data Fusion

Automatically ingests conflict event reports, satellite rainfall estimates, market price boards, and shipping route updates — then aligns them by district and week.

🔍 Explainable AI (OpenTSLM)

Built on OpenTSLM (Open Time Series Linear Models), the model shows each input’s contribution to the risk score. No black boxes.

🗺️ Geospatial Visualization

Interactive map interface (React-based) displays color-coded risk levels for every district. Click any district to see its forecast drivers.

📅 Forecast Horizons

View 4-week, 8-week, and 12-week outlooks. Plan interventions with a clear timeline.

🔄 How It Works

  1. Data collection: Every night, OpenRelief pulls new data from public APIs (conflict, weather, prices, shipping).
  2. Model training: The OpenTSLM engine trains a lightweight linear model per district, learning historical patterns.
  3. Forecast generation: The model predicts risk probabilities for the next 12 weeks.
  4. Explanation output: A simple bar chart shows which factor (conflict, rain, price, or shipping) pushed the risk up or down.
